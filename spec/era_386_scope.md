# Program scope through 386 and 68030 Macintosh

## Endpoint and comparison

The owner requested the complete reconstruction program through Intel 386. The PC endpoint includes original 80386DX and SX, with their distinct external buses. The Macintosh counterpart is the Motorola 68030 generation, including the earlier 68020 transition. This is an architecture/era comparison, not a claim of equal benchmark or clock performance. Apple lists the Macintosh II with a 68020, and the IIx with a 68030 and integrated PMMU; the Motorola manual documents that MMU.

386/68030 is a CPU-generation boundary, not a strict peripheral release-date cutoff. Explicit Sound Blaster profiles include later ISA cards useful on 386 hosts. 486, 68040, PowerPC and later CPU architectures require a scope decision. Existing earlier reconstruction work and independent Atari/C64/NES/SNES commitments remain in scope.

## Required complete-stack inventory

For every selected computer, inventory the sourced board revision and every required device, bus, card, storage/media interface, firmware/ROM, OS where applicable, development toolchain and representative software. Add missing chips or titles as research identifies them. Track original-software oracles, Rust implementations, SV-backed web demos and physical FPGA builds independently. The first issue inventory is not an exhaustive list of every historical semiconductor or software title.

## AdLib and Sound Blaster

AdLib OPL2 and the Sound Blaster 1.x/2.0/Pro/selected early 16 family are explicit deliverables. Use exact board/DSP/FM/mixer revisions. Original AdLib and early Sound Blaster OPL2, Pro variant differences and later OPL3/DAC paths need independent source qualification. Include DMA/IRQ and sampling timing, FM/music, PCM, mixer, and MIDI/game-port or optional CMS only where fitted. Digital audio equivalence, analog approximations, browser audio and physical FPGA output have separate evidence. A whole-program audio deliverable does not block an unrelated machine from passing POST.

## Macintosh progression

The initial machine inventory is 128K, 512K, Plus, SE, II, IIx, SE/30, IIci and IIfx. The 68000, 68020 and 68030 profiles share a compiler family but require exact instruction/ABI/runtime qualification. Source NuBus, PDS, SCC, SCSI, IWM/SWIM, ADB where fitted, video/audio/glue and IIfx-specific IOP devices. Do not assume 32-bit CPU hardware means every original ROM or System version is 32-bit clean. External 68851 is a selected 68020 MMU configuration; 68881/68882 FPU profiles remain distinct.

## PC and software progression

The selected PC scope includes IBM AT 5170, Compaq Deskpro 386DX, IBM PS/2 Model 25 SX and Model 35 SX (ISA), Model 70 386 and Model 80 (MCA), and Model 56 SLC (16-bit MCA). Include distinct Intel DX/SX, IBM 386SLC and AMD x86 profiles through Am386. Source exact motherboard/chipset, protected-mode/paging/virtual-8086 contracts, A20, cascaded PIC/DMA, RTC/CMOS, keyboard, storage and EGA/VGA. Model 80 supplies an actual MCA target; Model 30 remains ISA. Modern i686 code generation does not establish original i386 compatibility.

Catalog exact BIOS, DOS/Windows/OS2 and early Unix-like OS versions, diagnostics, drivers, languages, applications and games against hardware requirements. Mac software catalogs cover original ROM/Toolbox, System/Finder, drivers and applications with the classic Mac ABI; selected A/UX work needs its own hardware configuration. Reuse existing LLVM backends where qualified, and create per-title child issues after provenance/oracle research.

## Early Sound Blaster CMS path

The [[Chip] Philips SAA1099 family — CMS and early Sound Blaster audio](https://github.com/hevangel/ai_engineer/issues/182) is an explicit prerequisite of the Sound Blaster family. Source each board population rather than assuming CMS is either present or absent across all early cards.

The endpoint extends the broader historical reconstruction program; it is not a claim that every older CPU family, computer, card or software title has already been enumerated. The program coverage ledger must audit earlier families and create missing canonical issues as original catalogs and schematics establish their scope.

## Roland audio

The [Roland LAPC-I card](https://github.com/hevangel/ai_engineer/issues/183) adds an explicit ISA MPU-401/LA audio profile alongside AdLib and Sound Blaster. Inventory exact board/firmware, intelligent and UART interface behavior, CM-32L/MT-32 compatibility, independent audio evidence and asset provenance before sign-off. External MT-32 modules remain distinct from ISA cards; SCC-1/Sound Canvas requires a separate sourced card profile. The [shared Rust audio tracker](https://github.com/hevangel/ai_engineer/issues/141) includes Roland drivers and regressions and depends on this card for its Roland acceptance. Base-machine POST does not depend on optional audio. See the issue's manufacturer references; source coverage is not yet measured.

## Selected IBM 386 and AMD profiles

The owner replaced the Compaq Deskpro 386s SX target with **IBM PS/2 Model 25 SX** (#148/#149/#150) and a separate **Model 35 SX** track. Add **Model 70 386** for DX/MCA and **Model 56 SLC** for IBM 386SLC/16-bit MCA. The distinct 1986 Compaq 386DX and earlier Model 80 stay separate. Read [the sourced IBM/AMD scope](ibm_386_amd_scope.md) for years, exact machine profiles, canonical issues, photo limitations and CPU-to-machine selection.

AMD 8086/8088, 80186/80188, 80286/Am286 and Am386DX/SX have explicit history entries and coherent family issues. Early dates distinguish documented bounds and catalog advance information from shipping claims. These vendor additions do not imply AMD CPUs were factory-installed in the selected IBM systems.
